Deformity Correction Surgery (Bow Legs, Knock Knees, Limb Alignment)

వైకల్య సవరణ శస్త్రచికిత్స (వంకర కాళ్లు, నాక్ నీస్, అవయవ అమరిక)

Correction of bow legs, knock knees, limb length differences and bone deformities from old fractures, rickets or childhood conditions, using guided growth, osteotomy and external fixation.

Symptom Checklist & When to Consult

Visible bowing of the legs or knees touching while ankles stay apart
One leg shorter than the other, causing limping
Crooked limb after an old fracture that healed in a wrong position
Knee, hip or ankle pain from uneven weight bearing
Child with a deformity that is increasing as they grow

Treatment Options & Advanced Surgical Techniques

1. Assessment and Planning:

Standing full-length X-rays are used to measure the deformity and plan the exact correction.

2. Guided Growth in Children:

Small implants placed near the growth plate can gradually straighten the limb as the child grows, in suitable cases.

3. Osteotomy (Bone Realignment):

The bone is cut, realigned and fixed with a plate or nail to restore a straight axis. This can also reduce joint strain in adults.

4. External Fixation and Limb Lengthening:

For larger deformities or length differences, a frame can correct the bone gradually over weeks to months.

Patient Pre-Op Preparation Checklist

  • Standing full-length leg X-rays and other scans as advised
  • Blood tests and anaesthesia fitness; for children, vitamin D and calcium evaluation if rickets is suspected
  • Discuss realistic goals, the recovery period and school or work planning

Frequently Asked Questions

At what age can a child's bow legs or knock knees be corrected?

Mild changes are common and often settle with growth. Persistent or worsening deformity should be assessed, as growing children may be treated with guided growth.

Is deformity correction only for appearance?

No. Straightening the limb also improves walking and reduces uneven stress on the knee, hip and ankle joints.
